The Dink Diaries

Week of October 27, 2026

Four women. Four Florida pickleball towns. One very eventful week.

Patrice Waverly-Fontaine
Palm Beach, FL · Oct 27

Love-Love, Darling: When the Court Calls and the Champagne Answers

Well, my absolute darlings, what a week it has been. I barely had a moment to breathe between the courts, the club, and what I can only describe as the most scandalous doubles situation to unfold at Phipps Ocean Park since Bunny accidentally served into the adjacent yoga class last spring. More on that in a moment, because first I must tell you that Geoffrey has officially declared himself a pickleball widower, which I find both dramatic and deeply unfair given that he spent eleven years glued to Bloomberg terminals and I never once complained. Not once. Well, perhaps once.

Monday began with Carlos putting me through an absolutely punishing warmup that I am convinced was designed to humble me before a match. He had me doing lateral shuffles until my Lilly Pulitzer visor was completely askew, which simply cannot be the look. But darling, here is the thing about Carlos - he is always, infuriatingly, right. By the time Bunny arrived with her new JOOLA Ben Johns Hyperion paddle she found on Amazon, I was loose, focused, and ready to absolutely demolish the two women from the north end of the island who had been talking themselves up at Ta-boo all week. We heard through the most reliable channels, meaning Bunny overheard them at the bar on Thursday, that they had been taking private lessons and were, quote, coming for us. My darlings. They were not ready.

Nicolette Hargrove
Naples, FL · Oct 26

Dinks, Dinners, and Derek's Disaster at the Net (A Week Well Lived)

Honestly, there are weeks in Naples where everything just clicks into place like a perfectly placed third-shot drop, and this was absolutely one of them. The weather has been that dreamy kind of October perfection where the humidity finally lets go of you and the sky turns this ridiculous shade of turquoise that makes you feel like you are living inside a postcard. I cannot tell you how much I have missed mornings like this, pulling up to Pelican Bay courts with Rosie's nose hanging out the back window and my Coastal Hydration Bottle from Pickleball Florida USA already sweating in my hand, paired with my favorite Alo leggings that somehow manage to make me look like I have my life together even when I absolutely do not.

Monday started with an early session with Thomas, and I cannot stress enough how much having a real coach changes everything about your game. He has been working on my backhand roll volley for three weeks now and something finally clicked. I felt it in my body before I even saw it happen, that sensation where the paddle meets the ball with this satisfying low pop and the ball just skims the net tape and dies in the kitchen. Thomas actually stopped the drill and said Nicolette, that is the one, and I may have done a small embarrassing dance. Rosie, who I had tied to the fence post in the shade, lifted her head approvingly and then went immediately back to sleep, which honestly felt very on brand for her.

Vivian Kowalski-Reed
The Villages, FL · Oct 25

Kenny Called It An Unforced Error. I Called It Tuesday.

Honey, let me tell you about a week that had everything - bad dinks, good margaritas, and one man who absolutely should not be wearing a 4.5 badge like it is a medal of honor.

Monday started at Knudson Courts where Frank was already out there at 7am sharp, which means he was probably there at 6:52 judging the net height like it personally offended him. I rolled up in the golf cart around 8:15 wearing my new Coastal Serve Tank from Pickleball Florida USA and listen, I felt fast just putting it on. Lightweight, cute, wicks sweat like it has a personal vendetta against humidity. Marge saw me leave the driveway and yelled from her porch that I looked like I was going to a concert. Marge has not left her lanai voluntarily since 2019 so I took that as a compliment and kept driving.

Stella Marchetti
Sarasota, FL · Oct 24

I Came for the Sunshine, I Stayed for the Dinking, and Now I Can't Stop Talking About It

Look, I did not move from Brooklyn to Sarasota to become a pickleball person. I moved here for the light, the slower pace, and frankly because my nervous system was staging a full revolt against the L train. Six years later, I am out there four times a week making that satisfying little pop sound with a paddle like it is my actual job. I am not going to lie, Gabi is entirely responsible for this. My Brazilian neighbor showed up at my door one Saturday morning with two paddles and that enormous infectious laugh of hers and basically told me I had no choice. She was right. She usually is.

This week was a good one. Tuesday at Pompano Park, our regular crew of eight to ten showed up and the energy was sharp. There is something about that group that I genuinely love. Retired folks, working folks, some who have been playing for years and some who are still figuring out the kitchen line. Diane, my doubles partner, was locked in. She is a retired teacher and she plays exactly like you would expect a retired teacher to play, patient, strategic, zero drama, and quietly devastating when you underestimate her. We took two out of three sets and I felt borderline smug about it for the rest of the day.
