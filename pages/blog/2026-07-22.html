The Dink Diaries

Week of July 22, 2026

Four women. Four Florida pickleball towns. One very eventful week.

Patrice Waverly-Fontaine
Palm Beach, FL · Jul 22

Love-All and Loving It: When Pickleball Meets Palm Beach Perfection

Darlings, what an absolutely *breathless* week it has been on the courts. Between the humidity threatening my blowout and Bunny nearly causing a diplomatic incident at Phipps Ocean Park on Tuesday, I barely had time to collect myself before our Thursday session at The Bath & Tennis Club. I showed up in my new Coastal Serve Tank from Pickleball Florida USA — ivory, naturally, because I simply refuse to sweat in anything that does not pair beautifully with my Hermès court bag — and I could practically *feel* the other women reassessing their life choices. Geoffrey noticed from the terrace and gave me that approving little nod he reserves for particularly good wine vintages and my backhand. Both are improving, I will have you know.

Now, I must tell you about Tuesday, and I am asking you to keep this *just between us*, because what happened at Phipps is still being whispered about in certain circles. Bunny — bless her overconfident heart — convinced us to try a new mixed doubles arrangement with the Hartwell brothers, who had been absolutely *swaggering* around the courts all morning with their brand new JOOLA paddles, the ones everyone on Amazon seems to be raving about. I will admit the paddles are gorgeous and the control is remarkable, even on those Hartwell boys who have no business looking that confident. But here is the thing, darling — equipment does not replace footwork, and Carlos has been drilling that into me for three months now. Bunny, who refuses to listen to Carlos on principle, popped a shot so wide it nearly landed in someone's picnic. The Hartwells were gracious. I was *silently triumphant*.

Nicolette Hargrove
Naples, FL · Jul 21

Dinking, Dining, and Doing It All in Naples — A Week That Had Everything

Honestly, I do not know how it is already Friday because this week absolutely flew by in the most glorious way possible. Monday morning started the way all great weeks should — Rosie padding into the bedroom at six-thirty to remind me that the world is waiting, a cup of strong coffee on the lanai while the light was still doing that soft golden thing over the water, and the very real excitement of knowing Thomas had a full session planned for me at Pelican Bay. I pulled on my Coastal Serve Tank from Pickleball Florida USA paired with my favorite Alo high-waist leggings and honestly felt like I could take on anything. The tank fits so perfectly, moves with you, breathes beautifully in that Naples humidity that nobody warned me about when we moved here but that I now genuinely love in a complicated way.

Thomas was merciless in the best possible sense on Monday, and I cannot tell you how much my third-shot drop has improved since he started filming my footwork and showing it back to me. I cannot unsee what bad positioning looks like now, which is both a gift and a curse. We worked on transition zone movement for almost forty minutes and I left the court feeling that specific kind of tired that is really just pride wearing a different outfit. Derek texted me mid-session asking how it was going and I sent back a sweaty selfie, which he responded to with a single heart emoji because that man is economical with his words even though he is anything but economical on the court — bless him, he swings that paddle like he is trying to close a commercial real estate deal in one dramatic gesture and somehow he is having the time of his life doing it.

Vivian Kowalski-Reed
The Villages, FL · Jul 20

My Knees Are Fine, Kenny's Ego Is Not, and Dottie Almost Got Us Banned From Knudson

Honey, let me tell you about the week I just had. Monday started at five-forty-five when Frank rolled out of bed like a man on a mission, loaded up the golf cart with his paddle and his giant thermos, and was gone before I even found my left shoe. Seven AM sharp, every single day, rain or humidity so thick you could chew it. That man has played pickleball in conditions that would ground a small aircraft. I love him but I do not understand him.

Listen, Tuesday was my day at Knudson Courts and I pulled up in the golf cart feeling genuinely good about life. I had on my Coastal Serve Tank from Pickleball Florida USA and I am telling you that thing breathes like a dream in the Florida heat. No cotton disaster clinging to you by nine AM. I looked put together, I felt put together, and then I saw Kenny warming up on court three with that little strut he does and I thought oh here we go. Kenny, bless his heart, has been telling everyone he is a 4.5 since approximately the Obama administration. He is not a 4.5. He has never been a 4.5. His third shot drop goes about as far as my driveway and my driveway is short. I said nothing. Dottie said something. Dottie always says something.

Stella Marchetti
Sarasota, FL · Jul 19

I Came For The Dinking, I Stayed For The Drama (And The Prosciutto)

Tuesday at Pompano Park started the way it always does - Gabi arriving with the energy of a Brazilian carnival float, loudly announcing that she has been practicing her third shot drop and that everyone should be afraid. Nobody is afraid. We love her anyway. The Tuesday crew was fully assembled, eight of us plus two randos who wandered over from the tennis courts looking curious and slightly superior. By the end of the session they were asking about paddles. It happens every single time.

Here is the thing about this week - Marco actually asked to play. Voluntarily. Without me mentioning it first. I nearly dropped my coffee. He showed up at Urfer on Saturday, played four games, won three of them with that infuriating architectural precision he brings to literally everything, and then casually suggested we grab lunch at Piccolo Italian Market afterward like he has been a pickleball person his whole life. I watched him order a burrata and prosciutto sandwich and talk about erne shots with Diane and I thought, who even is this man. Diane, bless her, was steady as a metronome all week. That woman has a reset game that makes me want to cry in the best possible way.
