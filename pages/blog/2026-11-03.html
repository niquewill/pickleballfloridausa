The Dink Diaries

Week of November 3, 2026

Four women. Four Florida pickleball towns. One very eventful week.

Patrice Waverly-Fontaine
Palm Beach, FL · Nov 3

Aces, Aching Quads, and a Scandalous Serve at Phipps

Well, darlings, where do I even begin with this week. It was, as Geoffrey would say while barely looking up from the Financial Times, *eventful.* Monday started with Carlos arriving at seven sharp looking entirely too cheerful for that hour, announcing that we were doing lateral ladder drills before I had even finished my first espresso. I was wearing my new Coastal Serve Tank from Pickleball Florida USA — the one in that dreamy coastal sage — paired with my vintage Hermès silk hair wrap, because if you are going to sweat, you may as well do it with intention.

Tuesday at The Bath & Tennis Club was where things got truly interesting. Bunny and I were warming up on court three when a new doubles pair appeared, and I want you to know their footwork was *suspicious.* These women claimed to be 3.5 players but I watched their dinks and darling, those were not 3.5 dinks. Bunny leaned over to me between rallies and whispered something that I cannot fully repeat but it involved a certain round-robin tournament in Boca and a handicapping situation that raised more than a few eyebrows among those of us who were there. I am simply saying what was observed.

Nicolette Hargrove
Naples, FL · Nov 2

Dinks, Dinners, and Derek's Disaster at the Net (A Very Full Week in Naples)

Honestly, I do not know where this week went, but I can tell you it went fast and it went deliciously. We had one of those Naples weeks that feels like the universe conspired to give you absolutely everything all at once - scorching court time, laughing until my sides hurt, a husband who somehow managed to get his paddle stuck in the net (twice, Derek, twice), and meals that made me want to cry happy tears into my fork. If you are new here, welcome to my little corner of paradise where pickleball and pasta collide on a regular basis and nobody apologizes for either.

Monday started at Pelican Bay and I have to tell you, the courts were immaculate. There is something about that morning light coming through the palms at Pelican Bay that makes you feel like you are playing inside a postcard. I wore my Coastal Serve Tank from Pickleball Florida USA paired with my Alo high-waist leggings in that dusty sage green, and I cannot even tell you how many compliments I got before I even touched the ball. Thomas, my coach, was in rare form - he had me drilling cross-court dinks for a solid forty minutes and honestly my left forearm is still registering a complaint. He is relentless in the absolute best way. He kept saying Nicolette, stop reaching, stop reaching, let the ball come to you, and I kept nodding and then immediately reaching. We are working on it. Rosie was waiting in the car with the windows down and her head hanging out like a golden ambassador of joy, and she greeted me after practice like I had been gone for six months. I love that dog so much it is embarrassing.

Vivian Kowalski-Reed
The Villages, FL · Nov 1

Kenny Lost To A 73-Year-Old With A Knee Brace And I Have Receipts

Honey, let me tell you about a week that had everything - bad line calls, good margaritas, and one man's complete emotional collapse on court three at Knudson. Pull up a chair. Or a golf cart. Whatever gets you here faster.

Listen, Monday started the way all good weeks do, with Frank rolling out of the driveway at 6:47am because apparently 7am sharp means 6:47 in electrician time. I watched him go from the lanai with my Coastal Hydration Bottle from Pickleball Florida USA and a look of pure peace on my face, because Dottie wasn't picking me up until 8:30 and I had 45 blessed minutes to myself. That bottle keeps my water cold through three rounds of play in Florida heat and honestly it has saved more lives than my nursing career did, including my own on a Tuesday in August that I do not wish to discuss.

Stella Marchetti
Sarasota, FL · Oct 31

My Backhand Is a Work in Progress and So Is My Attitude

Here is the thing about pickleball in Sarasota in July - the humidity does not care about your plans, your paddle grip, or your dignity. I showed up to Pompano Park Tuesday morning looking like a functioning adult and left looking like I had swum there. The Tuesday crew was all present and accounted for - eight of us sweating through a rotation that somehow always ends with me and Diane grinding out points against two retired guys from Ohio who have entirely too much time to practice their drops. Diane is steady as a metronome. I am more like a jazz improvisation, which sounds cool until you realize jazz sometimes just sounds like noise.

I am not going to lie, I had one of those weeks where my backhand looked like I was swatting a mosquito with a frying pan. Marco came out Wednesday - reluctantly, as is his custom - watched me shanking thirds like I was allergic to success, and had the audacity to execute a perfect erne on his third point ever. He is an architect, so everything he does is structurally sound and aesthetically irritating. Gabi from next door, who is entirely responsible for this obsession consuming my life, texted me mid-game from the sideline: STELLA YOU LOOK LIKE YOU ARE FIGHTING THE BALL NOT HITTING IT. She was correct. She is always correct. She laughs while being correct, which is both her best and most infuriating quality.
