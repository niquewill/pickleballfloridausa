*The Week Bunny Nearly Caused an International Incident with Her Backhand*

Darlings, I simply must tell you about Tuesday at The Bath & Tennis Club, because honestly, I'm still recovering and it's already Friday. Bunny—my doubles partner and the only person in Palm Beach who can make a visor look both sporty and slightly dangerous—decided her new Selkirk Vela paddle needed to be "properly christened" against the Delancey sisters. Well. Let's just say things got rather heated when a questionable line call occurred, and I had to step in wearing my new Coastal Serve Tank (which, paired with my Hermès tennis bracelet, struck exactly the right note of "I'm athletic but also have a trust fund"). The paddle, by the way, is an absolute dream—something about the carbon fiber face makes even my mediocre serves look intentional. Geoffrey watched from the terrace with his Bloody Mary and later said it was "better than Wimbledon," which I think was a compliment?

The real drama, though, happened Thursday at Phipps when I arrived with my Coastal Court Tote—honestly the only bag capacious enough for my Lululemon change of clothes, my Coastal Hydration Bottle, and the emergency croissant from Cafe Boulud that Carlos doesn't know about (darling, he means well with his macro counting, but a girl needs carbs). I ran into Margaux, who was supposedly "working from home" but was clearly sneaking in a morning match. She tried to hide behind the pro shop, but I spotted her new K-Swiss Hypercourt Express shoes immediately—I'd been eyeing the same pair on Amazon just last week. We played a mother-daughter match that ended in a draw, though I suspect she let me win the last game because she felt guilty about the work-from-home fib. Now I'm having everyone over on Monday for what Bunny is calling "Pickleball and Prosecco," and I have a feeling someone might end up in the pool, fully clothed.

When Your Backhand Clicks The Burrata Calls Your Name

Honestly, I cannot believe it's already Thursday and I'm just now sitting down with my oat milk latte (Rosie's sweet face resting on my foot) to tell you about the absolute whirlwind this week has been on the courts! Tuesday morning at Naples Grande was one of those magical sessions where everything just clicked—Thomas has been working with me on my backhand dink for weeks, and suddenly it was like my paddle and I were speaking the same language. You know that feeling? When muscle memory finally takes over and you're not thinking anymore, just playing? Derek was there attempting his serves (bless him, he tries SO hard), and even he noticed the difference. We celebrated afterward with the most incredible burrata appetizer at Mediterrano—I'm talking creamy, cloud-like perfection with heirloom tomatoes that tasted like actual sunshine.

Wednesday's drill session at Pelican Bay was intense in the best way possible. Camille drove down from Atlanta for a long weekend (she's staying through Sunday, and I'm already planning our Mercato shopping trip), and we partnered up for some competitive games that had us both sweating through our gear. I've been living in my new Pickleball Florida USA performance skort paired with an Alo tank, and honestly, the moisture-wicking situation is a game-changer when you're playing three hours straight in this humidity. Between matches, I noticed one of the newer players struggling with her grip, so I recommended the Gamma Supreme Overgrip from Amazon—it's like twelve dollars for a three-pack and genuinely transforms how your paddle feels in your hand. Small investments, big returns! Now I'm eyeing Saturday morning's round robin and already thinking about whether we'll do lunch at The French on Fifth or maybe something lighter at Seed to Table, because Sunday Thomas wants to work on my serves and I cannot show up feeling heavy and sluggish.

Vivian Kowalski-Reed
The Villages, FL
Jun 21

Kenny's Meltdown, Dottie's New Paddle, Why I'm Never Wearing White Shorts Again

Honey, let me tell you about the week I've had. Monday started innocent enough—Frank dragged himself to his 7am game at Richmond like always, which gave me time to grab coffee at Katie Belle's before meeting Dottie at Knudson. Listen, I made the mistake of wearing white shorts. WHITE. At 68 years old, I should know better, but there I was, diving for a dink like I'm some kind of professional, and let's just say the red clay court and my backside became very well acquainted. Marge saw me pull into the driveway in my golf cart and actually came outside to stare. "Interesting look, Vivian," she says. The woman doesn't even play and she's got opinions about my mud-streaked behind. I smiled real big and told her it's called "effort," then drove off before she could respond.

Wednesday was the real show though. Kenny—KENNY—shows up at Knudson wearing his new Selkirk paddle like he's suddenly Ben Johns. Honey, this man still hits everything with his palm facing sideways, but now he's lecturing everyone about paddle technology. Dottie absolutely demolished him 11-3, 11-2, and he blamed the sun. THE SUN, which was behind him the entire time. Listen, after that humiliation, Dottie and I rewarded ourselves with lunch at Lake Sumter Landing, then I stopped at that Publix on Rolling Acres because we were out of everything. Frank had eaten all the good snacks again. I did grab myself one of those Pickleball Florida USA visors though—the one with the moisture-wicking band—because apparently I sweat like a construction worker now. Game changer for these humid mornings.

Stella Marchetti
Sarasota, FL

The Week My Third Shot Drop Actually Worked (And Other Miracles)

Here is the thing: I finally figured out why Marco's been giving me that look every time I line up for a third shot drop. Turns out I've been hitting it like I'm slapping sense into a design client who wants everything in Comic Sans. Tuesday morning at Pompano, Diane—bless her retired-teacher patience—spent twenty minutes showing me you're supposed to lift the ball, not murder it. Who knew? By Thursday at Urfer, I actually won a rally with one, and Marco looked at me like I'd just parallel parked perfectly on the first try. The man plays pickleball like he does everything else: reluctantly, competently, and with better form than people who actually care. I am not going to lie, it's infuriating.

Saturday I dragged Gabi to the Sarasota Farmers Market before our 9am at Bay Front, which was either brilliant or stupid depending on how you feel about playing pickleball on a stomach full of empanadas. She bought one of those Pickleball Florida USA visors—the navy one with the clean logo, thank god, not those tragic tie-dye situations some companies are pushing. Simple, readable, doesn't look like a Instagram filter threw up on your head. Anyway, we played terribly but laughed through three games, and I finally replaced my dead paddle grip with one of those Gamma Supreme overgrips from Amazon because my old one had the texture of a used yoga mat from 2003. The difference is obscene. Why did I wait this long? Pride? Laziness? Brooklyn stubbornness?
