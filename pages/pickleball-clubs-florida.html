Florida Pickleball Community

Pickleball Clubs & Open Play in Florida

Drop-in open play, indoor clubs and organized leagues across Florida's biggest pickleball metros.

The fastest way to plug into a Florida pickleball community is open play: show up, put your paddle in the stack and rotate in. From there, most players join a league or a club for regular games at their level. This guide covers the clubs, leagues and open play options in Florida's busiest pickleball metros.

Looking for a specific park? Every city has its own court guide with addresses and court counts.

Florida Pickleball Clubs & Open Play — FAQ

What is open play pickleball?
Open play is drop-in pickleball: players arrive, place their paddles in a queue and rotate onto courts for doubles games with whoever is next. It is the easiest way to meet players at a new park, and most Florida public courts and clubs post open play hours.
Where can I find open play pickleball in Miami?
Popular South Florida options include The Fort in Fort Lauderdale (43 courts), public courts at Flamingo Park in Miami Beach and Dink Club in Kendall for members and reserved play. Our Miami court guide lists more parks.
Where can I find open play pickleball in Tampa?
The City of Tampa runs pickleball at 13 outdoor parks and 6 indoor centers, including senior open play at Cuscaden Park. Indoor clubs like Pickleball Kingdom Pinellas Park and Ace Pickleball Club in Lutz offer drop-in play year-round.
Where can I find open play pickleball in Orlando?
The Picklr Orlando North in Apopka offers indoor open play and leagues, and Orlando Club Sport runs social pickleball leagues. See our Orlando court guide for public parks.
Are there pickleball leagues in South Florida?
Yes. The Fort in Fort Lauderdale and Dink Club in Miami both run leagues, and Silly Pickles organizes skill-matched social leagues in Miami and Fort Lauderdale.
How do I join a pickleball club in Florida?
Start with open play at a public park or a club drop-in session to find your level. Indoor clubs sell memberships with court reservations and leagues, while communities like The Villages and Naples have resident clubs and park-based programs.
